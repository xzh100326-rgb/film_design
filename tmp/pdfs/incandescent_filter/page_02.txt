Part 1. Reselection and mutation rate analysis 
In this section, we examine the effects of the reselection rate, 𝑟, and mutation rate,  𝜇, in 
the memetic algorithm optimization for both the radiative cooling device and the incandescent 
light bulb filter. In both cases, we study the performance of the memetic algorithm with 𝑟 =
[0.80, 0.90, 0.99] and 𝜇=[0.01, 0.05, 0.10].  
First, we perform the memetic algorithm optimization for the radiative cooling device. 
Aside from 𝑟 and 𝜇, all other optimization parameters are kept the same as those of the manuscript. 
For each combination of 𝑟 and 𝜇 listed above, we apply the memetic algorithm for 30 trials over 
105 generations. In each generation, for statistical robustness, we plot the median and inter-quartile 
range of these trials’ minimum merit functions (M.F.) in Fig. S1. As we can see, at a given 𝜇, a 
higher 𝑟 value generally speeds up the decrease in the merit function. For a small 𝜇= 0.01, a large 
𝑟 = 0.99 in general helps the algorithm to converge to a slightly more optimum structure. For a 
larger 𝜇= 0.10, a moderate 𝑟 = 0.90 helps the algorithm to converge towards a slightly more 
optimum structure. Furthermore, a low 𝑟 = 0.80 value produced relatively sub-optimal results but 
was more consistent, whereas a moderate 𝑟 = 0.90 value and a high 𝑟 = 0.99 value exhibit more 
spread in their resulting structures’ merit functions. These observations could indicate that the 
search landscape of the radiative cooling merit function could have a few prominent local minima 
that are high-performing. A high reselection rate helps the population to retain individuals at these 
prominent local optima, whereas a low reselection rate can more likely lose individuals at the 
prominent local minima and steer the population into search regions with less optimal local minima.
