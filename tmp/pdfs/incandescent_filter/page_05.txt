Finally, we investigate the effect of having a highly mutating population. We fix 𝑟 = 0.90, 
and we apply the memetic algorithm to both the radiative cooler and light bulb filter for 30 and 15 
trials, respectively, with 𝜇= [0.50, 0.80]. In Fig. S3, we plot the median and inter-quartile range 
of these trials’ minimum merit functions, along with the results from previous trials with 𝜇= 0.10 
for comparison. For the radiative cooling device plots in Fig. S3(a), we notice that while all three 
mutation rates show similar consistency, a mutation rate of 𝜇= 0.50 on average out-performed 
mutation rates of 𝜇= 0.10 and 𝜇= 0.80. On the other hand, for the light bulb filter plots in Fig. 
S3(b), we observe that having a very high mutation rate of 𝜇= 0.80 on average helps with the 
convergence to more optimal structures, which is consistent with the speculation that the search 
landscape for the light bulb filter is rugate and could benefit from high mutation that diversifies 
the search regions.
