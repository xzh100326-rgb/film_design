ABSTRACT  
The development of high -performance optical coatings demands advanced methods that reconcile the simultaneous 
optimization of multiple competing performance criteria —including spectrum, phase, group delay dispersion, and LIDT, 
with the imperative of ensuring manufacturability. Broad Band Optical Monitoring, a key high-precision in-situ monitoring 
technique, often introduces correlated thickness errors during deposition, directly compromising spectral performance and 
repeatability. To address these challenge s, we present a robust design method specifically for correlated BBOM errors, 
ensuring spectral stability under manufacturing variations.  The core of this method lies in utilizing an analytical model of 
production errors to predict the actual production yield of a coating design. By explicitly optimizing the design to maximize 
this predicted yield, the method ensures spectral stability under realistic manufacturing variations. Its effectiveness is 
validated through computational simulations. The results co nclusively show that these methods significantly enhance the 
comprehensive performance and production yield of optical coatings.  
Keywords: Design method, optical monitoring, robustness, muti-objective 
1. INTRODUCTION  
Optical coatings play a crucial role in modern industrial applications, serving as key components for achieving high -
performance optical devices. Their precise design is essential, as it directly impacts the overall performance and efficiency 
of these devi ces [1]. With the increasing demand for high -performance optical components, design objectives have 
expanded beyond conventional spectral responses to include phase, dispersion, optical loss, and laser -induced damage 
threshold (LIDT) [2]. These advanced re quirements impose stringent constraints on fabrication accuracy, making the 
control of manufacturing errors a critical challenge. Consequently, ensuring manufacturability, particularly the robustness 
against manufacturing errors, has become an increasingly important consideration [3].  
Robust design methods aim to enhance the optical performance of optical coatings against manufacturing errors. The 
primary goal of robust design is to minimize the sensitivity of the final optical performance to random and systematic 
variations in layer th icknesses and refractive indices, which are the most common manufacturing deviations. By 
incorporating statistical information about these errors into the design phase, robust optimization seeks to produce coatings 
with a high production yield—meaning a large percentage of fabricated samples will meet the required specifications. This 
shifts the focus from finding a single, theoretically perfect design to identifying a design that performs optimally across a  
range of probable manufacturing outcomes.  Existing robust design approaches mainly include methods like analytical 
approximation and Monte Carlo simulation. The first involves analytical approximation methods, which use a linear 
expansion of the merit function around the target design to estimate its sensitivity to small parameter variations. The 
second, more computationally intensive but accurate approach is the Monte Carlo method. Here, a large number of virtual 
coatings are generated by randomly perturbing the design parameters according to a predefined statistical distribution. The 
performance of these virtual samples is then statistically analyzed, typically by calculating the mathematical expectation 
of the merit function or directly estimating the production yield. These methods effectively improve coatings robustness 
by calculating the mathematical expectation of the merit function or production yield. However, they typically assume that 
layer-wise manufacturing errors are independent, an assumption that may not hold under advanced deposition

a fundamental challenge: it creates correlated errors. Unlike non -optical monitoring methods where thickness errors in

In this paper, a robust design method for correlated errors in broad band optical monitoring is proposed, and its 
robustness is validated through a series of computational manufacturing simulations.  
2. ROBUST DESIGN METHOD FOR BBOM  
In the robust design of optical coatings fabricated with broadband optical monitoring (BBOM), accurate error analysis is 
essential, as the optimization objective must explicitly account for the impact of manufacturing uncertainties on the final 
spectral performance. In particular, the correlated and cumulative nature of BBOM -induced errors makes their statistical 
characterization a critical prerequisite for robust optimization. Computational manufacturing provides a rigorous approach 
for such error analysis  by numerically simulating the full deposition process, including signal generation and spectral 
fitting. Through this process, both the thickness errors —along with their self -compensation effects —and the resulting 
spectral deviations can be accurately obt ained. However, due to its multi -stage iterative structure, computational 
manufacturing is computationally expensive and therefore unsuitable for robust design, where repeated evaluations are 
required during optimization.  To overcome this limitation, a fas t analytical method [4] is developed based on the 
underlying error propagation mechanisms in BBOM. This method enables efficient estimation of the statistical properties 
of correlated manufacturing errors and their impact on the optical spectrum .

We designed two broadband dielectric broadband beamsplitters for the 500–900 nm wavelength range at an incidence 
angle of 22.5° , based on the conventional method and the proposed robust method, respectively. The incident medium and 
substrate are air and Glass7980.Ta2O5 and SiO2 are chosen as the high and low refractive index materials respectively . 
The design target was to achieve equal reflectance and transmittance (R =T=50%) over the entire wavelength range under 
averaged polarization. Fig. 1 show the physical thickness distributions and the theoretical spectral performance of the two 
designs, respectively. The theoretical results indicate that both designs achieve near -ideal splitting performance, with the 
conventional design slightly outperforming the robust design in theory, while the subsequent computational manufacturing 
results demonstrate that the robust design achieves superior actual performance. 
 
Figure 2. Computational manufacturing simulation results for the (a) conventional design and the (b) robust design.  
To evaluate the practical manufacturing performance, 100 BBOM-based computational simulations were carried out 
for each design. The simulation parameters were set as follows: monitoring at a 0°  incident angle over a wavelength range 
of 400–1100 nm, spectral random error with a standard deviation of 0.5%, shutter delay with a standard deviation of 1 s, 
and a deposition rate of 0. 4 nm/s with a standard deviation of 0. 04 nm/s. Fig. 2 presents one of the simulated spectra. As 
shown in the figure, the robust design achieves a splitting ratio closer to the target value across the entire wavelength range 
compared to the conventional design. Statistical analysis based on the 100 simulations reveals that for the conventional 
design, the transmittance fluctuates significantly beyond the 45–55% range, with particularly pronounced deviations in the

3. CONCLUSION 
A robust design method for optical coatings fabricated with BBOM is presented, addressing correlated manufacturing 
errors. Using Taylor expansion, we derived analytical expressions for spectral expectation and standard deviation under 
correlated errors. A merit function incorporating both terms was constructed to minimize performance variability. 
Computational simulations of 50/50 beam -splitting coatings demonstrated that while the conventional design showed 
marginally superior theoretical performance, the robust design achieved better practical results with significantly narrower 
spectral envelopes, confirming reduced sensitivity to manufacturing errors. This method bridges the gap in robust design 
for BBOM-fabricated coatings.
